Is Your Business Ready for Growth — or Would More Work Cause Chaos?

For most small business owners, growth is the goal. But if your workload increased significantly next month, could your business actually cope with it?

For most small business owners, growth is the goal.

More customers. More enquiries. Bigger contracts. More money coming through the door.

But there’s another question worth asking:

If your workload increased significantly next month, could your business actually cope with it?

Growth is great — but if the foundations behind the business aren’t ready, more work can sometimes create more problems than opportunities.

What happens when a business grows faster than its systems?

When you’re starting out, it’s perfectly normal to have simple ways of doing things.

You might keep information in a spreadsheet, manage jobs from your inbox, remember important dates yourself or have one person who seems to know how everything works.

And while the business is small, that can work perfectly well.

The problem comes when the workload increases but the way you work doesn’t change with it.

Suddenly you might find:

  • invoices aren’t being raised quickly enough
  • enquiries or follow-ups are being missed
  • paperwork is piling up
  • employees aren’t clear about who’s responsible for what
  • the same information is being entered in several different places
  • everything still needs the business owner’s approval
  • important tasks are being completed at the last minute
  • you’re spending evenings catching up on admin rather than growing the business

None of these necessarily mean you’re running your business badly.

Sometimes they simply mean the business has outgrown the way it used to operate.

Would another big customer be exciting… or stressful?

It’s an interesting test.

Imagine you won a fantastic new customer tomorrow who increased your workload by 20%.

Would your first reaction be:

“Brilliant!”

Or would part of you immediately think:

“How on earth are we going to manage that?”

If it’s the second one, it might be worth looking at what’s happening behind the scenes before actively chasing more growth.

You don’t necessarily need complicated systems

Preparing a business for growth doesn’t mean buying expensive software or introducing layers of unnecessary procedures.

Often, the biggest improvements are much simpler.

It could mean:

  • clearly defining who is responsible for particular tasks
  • creating a straightforward process for new enquiries or customers
  • reviewing repetitive admin that could be simplified
  • setting up reminders for recurring deadlines
  • improving how information is stored and shared
  • creating templates for tasks you do regularly
  • reviewing whether certain work could be outsourced
  • making sure there is cover when key people are absent

The aim isn’t to create more administration.

It’s to make the administration you already have easier to manage.

Growth should create opportunity, not chaos

One of the best times to improve how your business operates is before you’re overwhelmed.

If something already takes too long, regularly gets forgotten or depends entirely on one person remembering to do it, adding more customers is unlikely to make it easier.

Taking some time to look at your processes, responsibilities and workload now can give you a much stronger foundation for whatever comes next.

And sometimes an outside pair of eyes can spot things you’ve simply become used to working around.
